About the series and its author

Why “Fusion in 5 Minutes” exists and who writes it.

1

The initiative

I have worked in fusion for years, and one thing worries me: almost all the information about it is in English, from scientific papers and industry reports to the news of every new record. Fusion is living its most exciting moment, with laboratory records, billions of dollars of private investment and a race among countries to build the first power plant. Meanwhile, more than 640 million Spanish speakers are watching from the outside.

I think of students in Peru and of Spanish speakers who could become the scientists and engineers of this industry but can't find where to start in their own language. I also think of the journalists, business leaders and policymakers who will have to make decisions about this technology without clear information at hand.

That's why I created Fusion in 5 Minutes (Fusión en 5 minutos). Every week I write an article in Spanish that explains a fusion topic with clear language, verified figures, everyday analogies and diagrams that help visualize the concepts. I also publish each article in English, so the conversation flows both ways. My goal is simple: knowledge about the energy of the stars should have no language barriers.

Analogy

If the conversation about fusion were a big conference, today almost half the talks would be in English and only 6 in 100 in Spanish. I want to be the interpreter in the booth for more than 640 million people.

2

The author

Humberto Torreblanca, PhDScientist, Educator and Author

I was lucky to be the last PhD student of Francis F. Chen, author of Introduction to Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion, the book from which generations of physicists learned about plasmas.

After earning my PhD at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), I worked at Intel Corporation, where I was responsible for plasma etchers and developed processes for manufacturing microprocessors. I then made the jump to nuclear fusion at the DIII-D National Fusion Facility, first in the operations group and then on the electron cyclotron heating (ECH) system. Later I moved to EPFL in Switzerland, where I worked at the FALCON facility with the gyrotrons for ITER. At Type One Energy I was a computational engineer and later led the design and implementation of the ECH system. Today I work at Helion Energy, where I run multiphysics simulations.

Since 2005 I have been actively involved in science outreach in the United States and Peru. I have also given talks about fusion and about graduate studies at several universities in Peru.
